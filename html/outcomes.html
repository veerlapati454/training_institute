AFTER THE CAPSTONE

What the twelve months after graduation actually look like

Placement support doesn't stop at the capstone panel — it runs on this rough schedule for every graduate.

MONTH 0

Capstone & resume lock-in

Your capstone becomes the anchor project on a resume built and reviewed with a career coach before you start applying anywhere.

MONTHS 1–2

Warm introductions first

Referrals into the hiring-partner network go out before generic applications — warm intros convert at a meaningfully higher rate.

MONTHS 2–4

Mock interviews & offer review

Weekly mock interviews with your coach, plus a second opinion from your mentor before you accept or negotiate any offer.

MONTHS 4–12

Still tracked, still supported

Graduates who haven't placed by month four get triaged into a focused catch-up track with extra coaching hours, not left to it alone.

HOW WE CALCULATE THIS

The numbers on this page, and exactly what's behind them

We'd rather you trust these figures because you can see how they're built, not because we ask you to.

Six-month reporting window

"Placed" means a full-time or equivalent-hours role, confirmed within six months of a graduate's capstone date — not lifetime placement.

Verified, not just claimed

Every placement is confirmed against an offer letter, a start-date confirmation, or a verifiable LinkedIn update — self-reports alone don't count.

91% survey response rate

Placement figures reflect the graduates we could confirm status for. Non-respondents are noted separately, not folded into the "placed" total.

What's excluded

Freelance, unpaid, and self-employment outcomes are tracked separately and never counted toward the placement rate shown here.

BEFORE YOU ENROLL

Questions about these numbers

A full-time or equivalent-hours role in or adjacent to your track, confirmed by an offer letter or start-date documentation, within six months of your capstone. Career-adjacent roles count; unrelated jobs taken to cover bills while still job-searching don't.

Graduates report their own starting salary, but we cross-check a sample against public offer bands and recruiter data each term. Figures shown are medians, which are less skewed by a handful of outlier offers than an average would be.

Career coaching and hiring-partner referrals continue through month twelve regardless. You're moved into a more intensive catch-up track around month four rather than left to search alone.

Yes — roughly six in ten graduates each cycle are career changers, and their placement outcomes are folded into the same figures shown here rather than reported separately.
