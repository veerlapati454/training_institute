HOW WE HIRE MENTORS

The bar someone has to clear before they review your work

Fewer than one in six applicants makes it through. Here's the actual process, not a marketing version of it.

1

Currently working the job

Applications open only to people actively employed in the discipline they'd teach — no former practitioners, no career instructors.

2

Technical bar-raiser round

A senior peer in the same discipline runs a working session on real problems — the same bar used for a hire on their own team.

3

Live teaching trial

Candidates review three anonymized rider submissions on camera. We're watching how they explain a mistake, not just whether they catch it.

4

Panel approval

Three current mentors and a program director vote before an offer goes out. Any single "no" sends the file back for another cycle.

WHAT MAKES REVIEW HERE DIFFERENT

The habits every mentor is held to

Review like a teammate, not a grader

Inline comments on the actual submission — the same review style a mentor would use on their own team, not a rubric checkbox exercise.

Stay working the job

Mentors are re-verified every cohort cycle. If someone stops working actively in the field, they stop mentoring in it until that changes.

One mentor, the whole track

Riders keep the same primary mentor start to finish, so feedback compounds across cycles instead of resetting with a new reviewer.

No single opinion decides readiness

Capstones get an independent second reviewer, and mentor performance itself is reviewed quarterly against rider feedback.

BEFORE YOU ENROLL

Questions about who's teaching

Every mentor holds a full-time role in the field they teach and mentors on top of that job. It's a deliberate tradeoff — you get someone whose judgment is current, in exchange for a smaller pool of available hours, which is why office hours and checkpoint turnaround are scheduled tightly around their calendars.

You can list a preference during enrollment and we'll try to match it, but assignment ultimately depends on mentor capacity that cycle. Every mentor on the roster clears the same bar, so match quality doesn't depend on landing a specific name.

It's rare, but if it happens the program director hands you off to another mentor in the same track within a week, along with your full feedback history so nothing gets lost in the transition.

Many do informally — several mentors have referred riders into openings at their own companies — but formal placement support runs through the separate career coaching team, not through your mentor relationship.

Every cohort cycle. Mentors are re-verified against the same bar new applicants clear, and a small number rotate out each term as their own roles or availability change.
